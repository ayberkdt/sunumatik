# Space-science visuals

## Orbit plot

State reference frame, epoch, units, central body, and whether the trajectory is osculating, propagated, simulated, or illustrative. Mark direction and key events. Do not imply scale accuracy when bodies and paths are diagrammatic.

## Ground track and map

Name the projection and coordinate system. Include longitude/latitude ticks and date range. Explain discontinuities at the antimeridian.

## Spectrum

Label wavelength/frequency/energy axis and units. Separate raw, calibrated, continuum-normalized, fitted, and residual data. State spectral resolution when relevant.

## Light curve

State time basis, normalization, detrending, cadence, uncertainty, and excluded intervals. Distinguish model and observation.

## Sky map

Name celestial frame and projection. State orientation and magnitude or intensity scale. Use perceptually appropriate colormaps.

## Mission architecture

Separate physical, functional, and information flows. Label interfaces, environments, and ownership. Use arrow styles consistently.

## Timeline and review chart

Separate planned, actual, uncertain, and decision-gated events. Show current date and schedule confidence when material.

## Image credits

Keep instrument, mission, processing, creator, license, and transformation metadata with the asset.
