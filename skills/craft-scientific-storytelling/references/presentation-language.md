# Presentation language is not report language

A slide deck is a SPOKEN medium with visual anchors. A report is a READ
medium with complete prose. Copying report sentences onto slides is the
single most common failure in scientific decks — the audience reads or
listens, never both. This reference defines the translation, with Turkish
examples because the decks are Turkish. It complements (does not repeat)
`write-assertive-slide-copy` (predicates), `enforce-slide-copy-density`
(word budgets), and `references/rhetorical-patterns.md` (tension patterns).

## The register shift

| Dimension | Report (yasak on slides) | Presentation (hedef) |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Edilgen: "ölçüm gerçekleştirilmiştir" | Etken: "ölçtük" / "KBRR ölçüyor" |
| Verb form | Nominalizasyon: "iyileştirme sağlanması" | Fiil: "iyileştiriyor" |
| Person | Kişisiz: "tarafımızca değerlendirilmiştir" | Sahipli: "değerlendirdik" |
| Sentence | Bağlı-cümle zinciri, 25+ kelime | Tek soluk, ≤12 kelime |
| Hedging | "olabileceği düşünülmektedir" | Kalibre tek fiil: "olabilir" / "gösteriyor" |
| Citations | Satır içi "[12, 14]" | Kaynak köşede sessiz künye; iddia serbest |
| Numbers | "%36,4218 oranında" | "üç kat" / "%36" — kesin değer notlarda |
| Connectives | "bu bağlamda", "söz konusu", "kapsamında" | Silinir ya da "burada", "bu yüzden" |

Test: read the slide aloud as a sentence you would actually SAY to a
colleague. If your mouth refuses it ("Sayısal kararlılık sınırlarının
değerlendirilmesi kapsamında..."), it is report language.

## Openings that work on stage

Never open with agenda ceremony ("Bu sunumda sırasıyla ... anlatılacaktır")
or a table of contents for talks under 20 minutes. Open with one of:

- **Phenomenon**: the thing itself, moving, before any definition.
  ("Ay'ın üstünden iki uydu geçiyor; aradaki mesafe mikron mikron titriyor.")
- **Number**: one startling, honest quantity. ("1,62 milyon katsayı.
  Üçgenin tepesinden bir satır silin — yörünge 147 metre kayıyor.")
- **Question the room owns**: a question the audience realizes they cannot
  answer. ("Bir yerçekimi modeli ne zaman yalan söylemeye başlar?")
- **Stakes**: what breaks if this goes unsolved. Decision-facing audiences.

The title slide may breathe (hero visual + title) — the FIRST content slide
carries the hook. Agenda, if truly needed, comes AFTER the hook, as one
line of section names, never numbered "İçindekiler".

## The spoken-beat contract

One slide = one spoken beat = one change in what the audience believes.

- The headline asserts the beat (assertion-evidence: claim on top, evidence
  as the body). If two claims fight for the headline, that is two slides.
- Visible text is the SKELETON of the beat; the presenter's sentence is the
  flesh. Slide text must be scannable in ≤5 seconds — the audience returns
  to listening before the presenter finishes the first sentence.
- Transitions are SPOKEN, not written. No "Şimdi de ... konusuna geçelim"
  on a slide; the speaker note carries the bridge ("Peki bu hatayı kim
  fark eder? → next slide").
- Every 4–6 slides, one breathing slide: a single image, number, or
  question — no list. Attention decays in ~10-minute waves; breathing
  slides and callbacks reset it.

## Callbacks and the through-line

Pick ONE concrete image/object/number in the opening (the coefficient
triangle, the 147 m gap, the twin satellites) and return to it at every
section boundary and in the close. The through-line is what the audience
retells afterwards. A deck with five equally-weighted motifs has none.

## Closings

The last content slide synthesizes: restate the through-line with the
evidence now attached, then ONE implication or decision ("N=1800'ü
kullanacaksanız, çift hassasiyet pazarlık konusu değil"). The thank-you
slide is a curtain, not a content slide — contact/credits only. Never end
on limitations, references, or "Sorular?" as the final claim; questions
are invited verbally over the closing visual.

## Speaker notes carry the nuance

Everything the register shift strips from the slide moves to notes, not to
the trash: exact values with uncertainties, hedges, citations, transitions,
anticipated Q&A ("İtiraz: SELENE zaten 150'ye çıkmıştı → cevap slayt 12'nin
notunda"). Notes are written in full spoken sentences — they are a script,
not a summary.

## Anti-patterns (block at review)

- Headline is a topic label ("Sonuçlar", "Yöntem", "Değerlendirme").
- Any sentence containing: gerçekleştirilmiştir, sağlanmaktadır,
  bulunmaktadır, söz konusu, kapsamında, hususunda, "yapılan çalışmada".
- A slide that only makes sense AFTER the presenter explains it.
- Reading the slide verbatim is the entire narration (slide = script ⇒
  one of the two is redundant).
- "Dinlediğiniz için teşekkürler" as the closing message of the argument.
- Ten slides in a row with the same rhythm (list → list → list). Alternate
  question / evidence / interpretation / consequence, per SKILL.md.
