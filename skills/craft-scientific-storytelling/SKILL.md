# Craft Scientific Storytelling

Create movement without distorting the science. A presentation story is an ordered change in audience understanding, not fictional decoration.

## Start from the insight brief

Confirm the audience, central question, ranked insights, evidence limits, and intended decision. If importance is unclear, use distill-scientific-insights first.

## Choose honest tension

Build interest from a knowledge gap, conflicting observations, scale difference, trade-off, failed expectation, methodological obstacle, or decision consequence.

Avoid shock language, clickbait questions, artificial secrecy, absolute superlatives, and claims such as revolutionary, unbelievable, or changes everything unless the evidence and context genuinely support them.

Read references/rhetorical-patterns.md for disciplined patterns and prohibited shortcuts.

## Design the change in understanding

For each section define:

1. what the audience currently assumes;
2. what question or difficulty appears;
3. what evidence changes the picture;
4. what the evidence means;
5. what remains uncertain;
6. why the next section is now necessary.

Alternate question, evidence, interpretation, and consequence. Do not repeat one rhetorical cadence on every slide.

## Write for speech

Treat each slide as one spoken beat. Use the slide for a headline, a key phrase, a number, a relationship, or a visual anchor. Keep explanation, nuance, and transitions in speaker notes.

Prefer specific verbs, concrete contrasts, and meaningful variation in sentence length. Avoid ceremonial openings, generic motivational language, and repeated phrases such as now let us look at.

## Deliver the story map

Return the opening move, section arcs, per-slide dramatic function, transition intent, reveal timing, callbacks, closing synthesis, and tone risks.

Pass phrasing to write-turkish-slide-copy or write-english-slide-copy. Pass visible text to enforce-slide-copy-density before design and implementation.
